1 · Canonical forms

A canonical term contains every variable exactly once. Retain the variable order in the question; the leftmost variable is the MSB.

m6 in ABC order: 110 → ABC′
M6 in ABC order: 110 → A′+B′+C

SOP: multiply a product by X+X′ for every missing X. Example: F=A+B′C in ABC order.

A(B+B′)(C+C′)=ABC+ABC′+AB′C+AB′C′
B′C(A+A′)=AB′C+A′B′C
F=Σm(1,4,5,6,7)=ΠM(0,2,3)

Remove the duplicate AB′C. POS: use T=(T+X)(T+X′) to insert a missing variable. Example: (A+B)=(A+B+C)(A+B+C′), so F=ΠM(0,1)=Σm(2,3,4,5,6,7).

Check: one-indices and zero-indices partition the full input range. The full minterm sum is 1; the full maxterm product is 0. An empty maxterm product is 1.

2 · K-maps that show the working

  1. Label axes in Gray order: 00, 01, 11, 10.
  2. Fill 1s/0s/X from the question. For SOP group 1s; for POS group 0s.
  3. Form the largest useful rectangles of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 cells. Opposite edges touch; diagonals do not.
  4. Keep only fixed variables in a group. In SOP a fixed 0 means prime; in POS a fixed 1 means prime.
  5. Select essential groups, then enough remaining groups to cover all required cells. X cells never need coverage.

Worked example: F=Σm(1,5,7,9,11,13,15). The three selected groups give:

F=C′D+AD+BD
  1. G1 · 1, 5, 9, 13 → C'D
  2. G2 · 9, 11, 13, 15 → AD
  3. G3 · 5, 7, 13, 15 → BD

Same colour = one group. Dashed pieces join across opposite edges. Small numbers are cell indices; X is optional. Overlapping groups are allowed.

The two pieces of C′D join across the left/right edges. A group of 2ᵐ cells removes m literals. Alternate minimum covers can be correct; compare their truth tables rather than just their appearance.

NAND: SOP → first-level NANDs → final NAND. NOR: POS → first-level NORs → final NOR. Include inversion gates unless complemented inputs are explicitly available. Respect a two-input or three-input gate restriction.

3 · Quine–McCluskey and the PI chart

  1. Write minterms and don’t-cares as binary; group by count of 1s.
  2. Combine patterns with exactly one differing fixed bit. Their dash positions must match. Mark both parents ✓.
  3. Repeat until no combinations remain. Uncombined useful patterns are prime implicants.
  4. Make the chart with required minterms only. Don’t-care columns are excluded.
  5. A column with a single tick identifies an essential PI. Select these first, then cover remaining columns.
0001 + 0101 → 0–01 → A′C′D
0–01 + 1–01 → ––01 → C′D

For Q376 the required columns are 1,5,7,9,11,13,15. C′D alone covers cell 1, BD alone covers cell 7 and AD alone covers cell 11: each is essential. Thus all three are selected.

Common error: “prime” does not automatically mean “essential.” Point to a column with exactly one tick to justify an EPI. For a minimum-expression question, show the cover selection after the chart.

For a design question: define inputs → truth table → canonical equations → K-map simplification → requested circuit. Half circuits have four rows; full circuits have eight. Borrow is 1 when B+Bin>A.

Two HA: P=A⊕B, c1=AB; S=P⊕Cin, c2=P·Cin; Cout=c1+c2.
Two HS: P=A⊕B, b1=A′B; D=P⊕Bin, b2=P′Bin; Bout=b1+b2.

Both constructions need an OR gate to join their carries or borrows. NAND-only/NOR-only answers must replace every XOR and inverter with permitted gates. Check the expanded circuits in the linked answers.

Ripple, serial and bit order

A general n-bit ripple adder uses n full adders; carry-out of stage i feeds carry-in of stage i+1. With no external carry-in, the LSB may use a half adder plus n−1 full adders. A serial adder reuses one full adder, with shift registers and a carry flip-flop, over n bit cycles.

Example: 1011₂+1111₂+1=11011₂. S3S2S1S0=1011 and C4=1. A0 is the LSB. Q533 prints an ambiguous reversed label order; its answer explains both interpretations.

5 · BCD: why the correction is 0110

A decimal digit uses only 0000–1001. The first binary adder gives C4 and Z3Z2Z1Z0; the correction circuit detects a value above 9.

K=C4+Z3Z2+Z3Z1
Correction word=0KK0
Units=(Z+6K) mod 16; decimal carry=K
ExampleRaw sumKCorrectionBCD answer
3+40011100111+0000=001110000 0111
7+91000010000+0110=001100001 0110
9+81000110001+0110=001110001 0111
8+70111111111+0110=101010001 0101

Critical detail: in 7+9 the second adder produces no carry, but the decimal answer still carries. Take the decimal carry from K.
